# Thermodynamics II — Integrated Energy Systems Project

**Course:** Thermodynamics II  
**Institution:** Kansas State University  
**Term:** Fall 2023  

---

## Project Overview
This project involved the design and analysis of an integrated energy system to support a remote research facility in Antarctica. The system was required to simultaneously provide electrical power, potable water, and conditioned air under extreme environmental conditions.

The design combined a Rankine power cycle, combustion analysis, heat exchangers, and HVAC subsystems into a single, closed-form thermodynamic solution.

---

## Technical Scope
- Designed a Rankine cycle power system with realistic component efficiencies and heat losses
- Performed combustion analysis for propane fuel, including annual fuel consumption
- Sized water heating and cooling loops to meet daily usage requirements
- Analyzed HVAC heating and ventilation loads for extreme cold-weather operation
- Evaluated system thermal efficiency, safety considerations, and feasibility

All calculations were performed using first- and second-law thermodynamic principles, supported by T–s and P–v diagrams.

---

## Key Outcomes
- Electrical power generation sufficient for a 75-person facility  
- Hot and cold water supply meeting daily usage requirements  
- HVAC system sized for both design-day and average Antarctic conditions  
- Integrated system efficiency and safety assessment  

---

## Skills Demonstrated
- Advanced thermodynamic cycle analysis  
- Energy systems integration  
- Combustion and heat exchanger modeling  
- Engineering assumptions and feasibility evaluation
