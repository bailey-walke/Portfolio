## Overview
This folder documents my systems engineering work on two student-led CubeSat missions developed through Kansas State University’s interdisciplinary CubeSat program:

- **NuKAT** — a neutron detector technology demonstration mission  
- **PHAT-Sat** — a hyperspectral Earth-observation mission for precision agriculture  

Both missions follow **NASA-style systems engineering practices**, including formal design reviews, requirements development and verification, mission concept-of-operations (CONOPS), and subsystem integration.

---

## Missions

### NuKAT — *Neutron Detector: K-State Analysis Tool*
**Configuration:** 2U CubeSat  
**Mission Type:** Technology Demonstration  

NuKAT is designed to space-qualify the DSC-Domino neutron detector platform, advancing it from TRL-6 toward TRL-7/8. The mission measures neutron flux across thermal, epithermal, and fast energy regimes in Low Earth Orbit, with particular focus on high-radiation regions such as the South Atlantic Anomaly and polar passes.

---

### PHAT-Sat — *Precision Hyperspectral Agricultural Tracker Satellite*
**Configuration:** 3U CubeSat  
**Mission Type:** Earth Observation  

PHAT-Sat is a precision agriculture mission designed to collect hyperspectral imagery of the U.S. Midwest from a sun-synchronous Low Earth Orbit. The mission targets improved revisit rates and spectral resolution relative to existing public datasets and supports onboard processing to generate vegetation and soil health products.

## Major Design Reviews
**Systems Requirements Review (SRR)**
- Established mission objectives, success criteria, and system-level requirements.
- Developed initial requirements traceability framework and subsystem allocations.

**Mission Definition Review (MDR)**
- Conducted mission architecture trades, payload assessments, and preliminary subsystem selections.
- Refined system budgets and mission operations concepts.

**Preliminary Design Review (PDR)**
- Presented spacecraft architecture, subsystem designs, risk assessments, verification plans, and mission budgets to industry and university reviewers.
- Demonstrated design maturity and readiness to proceed toward detailed design and Critical Design Review (CDR).

## My Role & Contributions

Across both missions, I served in a systems engineering leadership role, responsible for guiding the satellite design from early concept through formal design reviews.

Key contributions include:
- Led mission and system architecture definition
- Developed Concept of Operations (CONOPS), mission phases, and operational modes
- Defined system block diagrams, subsystem interfaces, and cabling/harnessing concepts
- Authored and managed mission and system-level requirements
- Built and maintained Requirements Verification Matrices (RVMs)
- Defined modes and states, including safe, commissioning, nominal operations, and survival modes
- Coordinated work across mechanical, electrical, software, payload, and ground system teams
- Presented mission designs at formal reviews (MDR, pre-PDR) aligned with NASA CubeSat standards

## Systems Engineering Focus Areas
- Developed and maintained mission requirements, Interface Control Documents (ICDs), Concept of Operations (CONOPS), and system-level architecture documentation.
- Led weekly systems engineering and leadership meetings across all spacecraft subsystems.
- Directed spacecraft configuration development including subsystem packaging, deployable systems, mechanical integration, and design for manufacturing and assembly.
- Established verification and validation strategies to maintain requirements traceability and subsystem compatibility throughout the design lifecycle.
- Managed technical risk, schedules, and cross-functional interfaces to maintain program execution and design maturity.
- Coordinated system-level trades involving mass, power, communications, thermal, and mission performance requirements.
- Applied CubeSat Design Specification (CDS), SSCMAN, NASA GEVS, NASA CSLI, and launch provider requirements during mission development and review activities.
